Open. Paint. Export.

The same multi-material paint workflow you know from the desktop slicer, sized for a touchscreen.

01

Open any project

Plates, objects and multi-part assemblies all load. Pick the exact part you want to paint from the object list.

02

Paint with real filaments

Brush or Smart Fill using the filaments your project declares, with undo per stroke. Each part keeps its edits as you switch.

03

Export and slice

Save a 3MF that Bambu Studio opens as its own project — settings, plates and metadata exactly as they were.

Brush

Paint follows the brush, not the mesh.

Triangles the brush only partly covers are split finely, so a small dab on a big flat face paints a clean disc. Fast strokes stay continuous.

Smart Fill

One tap, stopped at sharp edges.

Flood a whole face in one tap. Edge detection outlines where fills and strokes will stop, updating live as you change the angle.

Filaments

Colors come from your project.

The palette shows the filaments your file actually uses. Recolor one with a long-press, or add a new one based on an existing preset so it keeps the right material settings.

Apple Pencil

Made for iPad.

Pressure changes brush size. Double-tap switches tools. Hover previews the brush or fill before you touch down, and your palm is ignored while drawing. Open several projects side by side in separate windows.

Your file stays your file.

FilaTint never rebuilds your project. It changes only the paint on triangles you repainted, and writes everything else back byte-for-byte. Paint done in Bambu Studio loads here as painted, and survives a round-trip.

!

Added a filament? Check purge volumes.

New color pairs get conservative purge values. Before a multi-color print, open the project in Bambu Studio and recalculate flushing volumes.
